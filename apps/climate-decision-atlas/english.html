Two meters at a fish processing plant

In Norway, two meters recorded the power used by a refrigeration system and an electric boiler for 240 hours, from 5 to 14 January 2021. The map shows the first day, using the times in the original file. The buildings and pipes illustrate how heat might travel.

Hours ranked by cooling power useHoursAverage boiler power
Highest quarter6022.82 kW
Lowest quarter60217.53 kW

Cooling and water heating often drew the most power at different times. Could a tank save heat from one shift for the next? To find out, we would need water temperatures, flow rates, hot-water use and tank readings. The boiler meter measures the whole boiler, so we cannot tell how much hot water went to cleaning.

Two production periods at Hardwick, Australia

Using the heat output and annualised operating hours in the project report, we estimated 6,978 MWh of heat per year in the first period and 4,653 MWh in the second. Shorter running time accounts for about 62.5% of the calculated drop. Seasons and production schedules also changed, so this comparison cannot tell us what caused the shorter hours.

Look back and learn

ClimateOS helps people see how their choices affect the environment, take part in decisions and learn together from what actually happens.

Imagine a night-shift worker asking, “Could the heat from the cold store help warm our wash water tonight?” Use the map to find where heat comes from and who could use it. Then compare the timing and check the records. The people who work here may spot something the meters cannot explain.
