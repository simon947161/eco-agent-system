# Radar Principles

## Not All Changes Matter

Radar should help identify change signals, but not every change requires
attention or governance response.

## Relationships Matter More Than Isolated Events

An isolated event becomes more meaningful when interpreted through
relationships, system context, history, and evidence.

## Change Requires Context

A change signal needs location, time, baseline, source, relationship context,
and uncertainty.

## Change Requires Comparison

Change is understood through comparison: before and after, site to site,
baseline to alternative, expected to observed, or relationship to relationship.

## Change Requires Validation

Radar can identify a signal. Validation is required before treating the signal
as true, material, or decision-ready.

## Human Review Remains Central

Humans decide whether a radar signal becomes evidence, a project concern, a
governance item, or no action.

## Limits

These principles do not create monitoring, alerting, forecasting, or automated
decision systems.
