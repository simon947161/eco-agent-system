# Climate Zone Relationships

## Purpose

Climate relationships vary by location. ClimateOS should not assume that one
relationship behaves the same everywhere.

## Mountain Systems

Mountain systems may show strong elevation, snow, frost, rainfall, forest,
water-source, and temperature-gradient relationships.

## Dryland Systems

Dryland systems may show strong relationships between rainfall timing, soil
moisture, heat, vegetation stress, wind, and recovery.

## Agricultural Systems

Agricultural systems may show relationships between land management, water
availability, crop condition, soil health, carbon context, and community
decision-making.

## Urban Systems

Urban systems may show relationships between heat, built surfaces, drainage,
vegetation, energy demand, air quality, and governance.

## Relationship to CCZPS

CCZPS concepts can help describe climate-zone planning and sensitivity, but
this relationship layer does not implement CCZPS runtime or calculations.

## Limits

Climate zone descriptions are conceptual and require observation, evidence,
validation, and local review.
