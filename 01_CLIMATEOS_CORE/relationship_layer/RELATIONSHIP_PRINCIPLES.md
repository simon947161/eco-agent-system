# Relationship Principles

## Everything Interacts

Climate, water, land, vegetation, biodiversity, human activity, and governance
are connected. ClimateOS should preserve those connections rather than
separate every signal into isolated records.

## Relationships Are Dynamic

Relationships change across seasons, disturbances, recovery periods, and human
management actions.

## Relationships Vary by Climate Zone

The same relationship may behave differently in mountain, dryland,
agricultural, or urban systems.

## Relationships May Strengthen or Weaken

A relationship can become stronger, weaker, more unstable, or harder to detect
over time.

## Relationships Require Evidence

Relationship statements should be connected to observations, evidence
candidates, reviewed evidence, and uncertainty notes.

## Relationships Require Validation

Relationship interpretations need review. A relationship note is not validated
until evidence and assumptions are checked.

## Relationships Need Governance Context

Some relationships affect decisions, accountability, and risk. Governance
records should preserve how a relationship is interpreted and used.

## Limits

These principles do not create models, predictions, recommendations, or
automated decisions.
