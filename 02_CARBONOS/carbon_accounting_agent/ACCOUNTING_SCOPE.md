# Carbon Accounting Scope

## Purpose

This document defines the future accounting activities the agent may organise.
Documentation of an area does not mean it is implemented.

## In Scope

| Area | Conceptual coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Energy Consumption | Quantity, unit, source, period, boundary, and evidence |
| Electricity Use | Consumption records and reviewed green-power classifications |
| Fuel Consumption | Fuel type, amount, unit, use, source, period, and evidence |
| Emission Records | Future derived records with method, factor reference, uncertainty, and review status |
| Operational Activities | Evidence-bearing activities relevant to a declared inventory |
| Carbon Inventories | Structured collections of reviewed records for a boundary and period |
| Product-Level Information | Activity and emission information under a declared product boundary |
| Projects | Bounded project activities, assumptions, periods, and records |
| Scenarios | Clearly labelled hypothetical inputs and accounting assumptions |
| Review Preparation | Evidence summaries, gaps, validation findings, and reviewer notes |

## Accounting Categories

Future tasks may organise Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 categories only when
their definitions, methods, boundaries, evidence, and limitations are explicit.
Task52 does not select standards or implement category logic.

## Out of Scope

- financial accounting, audit opinions, or financial reporting;
- carbon trading execution, brokerage, settlement, or recommendations;
- regulatory enforcement, filing, approval, or legal interpretation;
- automated compliance, assurance, certification, or claim decisions;
- emission factor libraries or factor selection;
- carbon or greenhouse-gas calculations in Task52;
- external database, registry, meter, supplier, or API connections; and
- autonomous operational or investment recommendations.

## Interpretation

The agent supports preparation and review of carbon accounting information.
Qualified humans remain responsible for applicable standards, methods,
factors, conclusions, claims, and real-world use.
