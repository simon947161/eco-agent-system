# Carbon Accounting Example Scenarios

## Purpose

These scenarios illustrate conceptual records and review outcomes only. They
contain no emission factors or calculations.

## Scenario 1: Single Facility Electricity Use

**Inputs:** facility boundary, accounting period, meter records, invoices, and
a reviewed green-power classification.

**Conceptual outcome:** an energy record linked to its boundary, evidence, and
classification, ready for future method and factor work. Missing evidence is
listed rather than estimated.

## Scenario 2: Mixed Electricity Sources

**Inputs:** on-site generation, grid electricity, certificates, consumption
records, and Task51 results with different statuses.

**Conceptual outcome:** separate energy records preserve `Physical`, `Trading`,
`Allocation`, `Unknown`, or `Needs Review` status. No quantities are combined
or emissions calculated in Task52.

## Scenario 3: Industrial Site Energy Records

**Inputs:** electricity, multiple fuels, operating activities, invoices,
meters, internal reports, and a facility boundary.

**Conceptual outcome:** evidence-bearing activity and energy records grouped
by period with gaps, units, sources, assumptions, and review needs identified.

## Scenario 4: Multi-Facility Organisation

**Inputs:** organisational boundary, several facilities, local periods,
activity records, classifications, and exclusions.

**Conceptual outcome:** facility records remain distinct while a conceptual
inventory structure records consolidation boundary, versions, missing sites,
and unresolved inconsistencies.

## Scenario 5: Incomplete Accounting Evidence

**Inputs:** partial invoices, estimated fuel data, missing meter intervals, and
an electricity classification marked `Needs Review`.

**Conceptual outcome:** accounting preparation remains incomplete. The output
lists evidence gaps, uncertainty, affected records, responsible owners, and
required human review. It does not invent values or claim a complete inventory.

## Limitations

Real accounting requires approved methods, factors, standards, evidence, and
qualified review. These examples do not establish comparability, compliance,
assurance, or environmental performance.
