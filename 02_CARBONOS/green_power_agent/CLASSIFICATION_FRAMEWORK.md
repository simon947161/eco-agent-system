# Green Power Classification Framework

## Purpose

This conceptual framework describes attribution pathways for organising green
power evidence. Categories are review labels, not regulatory findings or legal
interpretations.

## Physical Recognition

### Definition

Electricity attributes are proposed for recognition because the reporting
entity has a documented physical generation, delivery, or direct consumption
relationship with the electricity source.

### Typical Evidence

- on-site generation and consumption meter records;
- asset ownership or operational-control records;
- dedicated supply or connection documentation;
- time and location records for generation and consumption; and
- contracts describing physical delivery.

### Limitations

Physical proximity alone does not prove ownership of environmental attributes.
Grid delivery, exports, shared assets, contractual transfers, and certificate
issuance may affect interpretation.

### Review Considerations

Confirm the facility boundary, asset relationship, reporting period, measured
quantity, imports and exports, attribute ownership, and absence of conflicting
claims.

## Trading Recognition

### Definition

Electricity attributes are proposed for recognition through a documented
purchase, transfer, retirement, or other transaction-based pathway.

### Typical Evidence

- electricity supply or power purchase agreements;
- invoices and transaction records;
- green certificate ownership and retirement records;
- registry references or supplier attestations; and
- records linking quantities and periods to consumption.

### Limitations

A commercial label or payment does not by itself establish attribute
ownership, eligibility, retirement, geographic matching, temporal matching,
or freedom from double counting.

### Review Considerations

Check parties, rights transferred, certificate status, quantities, units,
dates, geography, reporting method, duplicate claims, and the authority of
the evidence source.

## Allocation Recognition

### Definition

Electricity attributes are proposed for recognition because a documented
allocation method assigns shared, portfolio, supplier, or organisational
attributes to a reporting unit.

### Typical Evidence

- approved allocation policies and calculation descriptions;
- portfolio or supplier electricity records;
- facility, department, product, or tenant allocation records;
- reconciliation tables; and
- reviewer approval and version history.

### Limitations

Allocation may depend on assumptions and may not represent physical delivery
or a separate market transaction. Poorly defined denominators, overlapping
allocations, or changing boundaries can produce inconsistent claims.

### Review Considerations

Confirm the allocation population, method, period, quantities, exclusions,
reconciliation, version, decision owner, uncertainty, and absence of duplicate
allocation.

## Unknown Classification

### Definition

Available information does not support a defensible proposal for physical,
trading, or allocation recognition.

### Typical Evidence

- incomplete meter or invoice records;
- unsupported labels such as "green" or "renewable";
- conflicting contracts or certificates;
- evidence outside the consumption period; or
- records with unknown provenance or ownership.

### Limitations

`Unknown` does not mean the electricity is non-green. It means the current
evidence is insufficient for classification within this framework.

### Review Considerations

Record missing evidence, conflicts, uncertainty, responsible owner, and the
specific information needed for further review. Do not silently select a more
favorable category.

## Multiple Pathways

A consumption record may have evidence associated with more than one pathway.
Future logic must define whether classifications can coexist and how priority
or overlap is handled. Until then, potentially competing pathways should be
marked `Needs Review`.
