## Status

Reserved future task.

## Purpose

Task 100 will introduce the foundation for a future ClimateOS Stewardship Layer.

The purpose is to extend ClimateOS beyond planning, evidence, validation, governance, and reporting into long-term environmental maintenance, care, adaptation, recovery, and managed transition.

This task should only be started after the core CCZPS-Lite kernel has matured through earlier releases and after Scenario, Evidence, Hypothesis, Review, and Report structures are stable.

---

## Background

Current CCZPS-Lite development focuses on:

- scenario intake;
- evidence organisation;
- meteorology evidence;
- planning hypotheses;
- validation support;
- evidence traceability;
- internal governance decision support;
- scenario comparison;
- human-readable dashboard output;
- consolidated scenario, evidence, and governance review packs.

These are foundations for planning support.

The future Stewardship Layer should address a different question:

What happens after planning, damage, disruption, or environmental stress has already begun?

Instead of asking only how to predict or approve actions, the Stewardship Layer should ask how a place can be monitored, maintained, adapted, recovered, or transitioned with dignity over time.

---

## Core Framing

ClimateOS should not be framed as a system that saves the world by itself.

It should be understood as an environmental stewardship operating system: a way to organise evidence, care, maintenance, adaptation, review, memory, and responsibility in a time when many landscapes and communities may already be under severe stress.

The future Stewardship Layer should support long-term care rather than one-time technical conclusions.

## Stewardship Principles

Task 100 should preserve and operationalise the following principles where appropriate:

### Life First

Protect life before optimising systems.

### Function Before Optimisation

Preserve basic system function before attempting optimisation.

### Avoid Irreversible Loss

Flag and carefully review irreversible ecological, cultural, hydrological, or social losses.

### Preserve Future Options

Prefer actions that keep future recovery, adaptation, or restoration pathways open.

### Evidence Before Action

Ground stewardship actions in evidence, uncertainty, and review status.

### Human Review Required

ClimateOS should support human judgement, not replace it.

### Long-Term Stewardship

The goal is not one-time victory. The deeper goal is long-term care, monitoring, adaptation, and responsibility.

---

## Environmental Palliative Care and Graceful Transition

Task 100 may also explore a difficult but important boundary: some systems may not be fully recoverable within available time, resources, or climate conditions.

In such cases, ClimateOS should not automatically abandon the system. Instead, future stewardship logic may support:

- protecting remaining ecological function;
- preserving seeds, data, stories, and local knowledge;
- supporting managed retreat where necessary;
- recording what was lost and why;
- protecting cultural and community memory;
- retaining future recovery options where possible;
- helping communities transition with dignity rather than denial.

This must remain human-review-centred and should never become an automatic retreat, closure, or abandonment engine.

Scenario → Evidence → Hypothesis → Review → Report → Stewardship

The Stewardship Layer should not replace the existing five-object model. It should extend it into long-term care and maintenance.

## Relationship to Future Application Layers

Task 100 may support future systems such as:

- FarmerOS;
- GardenOS;
- WaterOS;
- Building Climate OS;
- Dryland Restoration OS;
- broader ClimateOS.

In each case, stewardship may mean different things:

- a farm may need soil, water, crop, and livelihood maintenance;
- a garden may need microclimate and biodiversity care;
- a watershed may need flow, recharge, and ecosystem-function monitoring;
- a building may need climate-adaptive operation and passive-system maintenance;
- a dryland restoration system may need long-term vegetation, soil, water, and community care.

---

## Safety Boundaries

Do not implement Task 100 until the repository owner explicitly requests it.

When eventually implemented, Task 100 must not introduce:

- automatic retreat decisions;
- automatic abandonment decisions;
- automatic closure decisions;
- automatic approval decisions;
- legal determinations;
- regulatory determinations;
- engineering conclusions;
- medical or psychological advice;
- financial or investment recommendations;
- irreversible transition decisions without human review.

Human and professional review must remain mandatory.

---

## Future Acceptance Criteria

Task 100 may be considered complete only when a future implementation or planning document:

- defines the Stewardship Layer clearly;
- distinguishes maintenance, recovery, adaptation, and managed transition;
- preserves human and professional review boundaries;
- avoids automatic retreat or abandonment decisions;
- connects stewardship records to Scenario, Evidence, Hypothesis, Review, and Report;
- documents safety boundaries;
- provides deterministic local outputs if implemented;
- passes all repository tests;
- creates a Draft PR for review.
