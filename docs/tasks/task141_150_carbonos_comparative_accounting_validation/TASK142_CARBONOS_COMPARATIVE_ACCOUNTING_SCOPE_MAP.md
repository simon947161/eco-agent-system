# Task142 CarbonOS Comparative Accounting Scope Map

## Purpose

Task142 defines the scope map for CarbonOS comparative accounting architecture within the Task141-150 CarbonOS Comparative Accounting and Climate Validation Architecture Prototype Sprint.

This is documentation-only architecture work. It defines what CarbonOS may compare, what it may not claim, and how later research and prototype tasks must remain bounded before any operational capability is considered.

Task142 does not create runtime, API, MCP, database, scoring, automation, calculators, standards interpretation, compliance output, assurance output, certification output, public disclosure conclusion, or real company / project carbon conclusion capability.

## Scope Map Definition

A CarbonOS comparative accounting scope map is a non-operational architecture artifact that identifies comparison dimensions, review boundaries, and linkage questions for carbon accounting methods, evidence structures, claim types, and validation contexts.

The scope map is not:

- a standards guide
- a compliance tool
- an assurance tool
- a certification tool
- a disclosure engine
- a calculator
- a scoring model
- a database schema
- an API contract
- an MCP design
- an automated workflow

The scope map is a governance boundary for later tasks. It helps future work ask disciplined comparison questions without treating CarbonOS as a jurisdictional authority or standards interpreter.

## What CarbonOS May Compare

CarbonOS may compare non-authoritative structures such as:

- how a carbon accounting question defines its boundary type
- what evidence category is associated with a claim or method
- whether a claim is framed as inventory, reduction, removal, offset, transition, risk, disclosure, or validation context
- whether a method, formula, factor, or assumption is referenced, missing, disputed, versioned, or pending research
- whether supporting evidence has been internally reviewed, expert-reviewed, externally assured, disputed, superseded, or not yet reviewed
- which jurisdictional or framework context a future research protocol may examine
- what uncertainty, limitation, dependency, or assumption affects interpretation
- how a comparative accounting output may connect to ClimateOS / EcoEngine validation questions about real-world climate, ecological, physical-system, or resilience effects

These comparisons must remain descriptive and review-oriented. They may identify structural differences, evidence gaps, open questions, and validation needs. They may not decide legal compliance, assurance status, certification status, or factual climate impact.

## What CarbonOS May Not Claim

CarbonOS may not claim that:

- a company, project, asset, product, jurisdiction, disclosure, or intervention is compliant
- a claim is assured, verified, certified, approved, or legally sufficient
- a method is the correct method under any specific standard
- a formula or factor is authoritative for a jurisdiction or framework
- a disclosure satisfies IPCC, ISSB, IFRS S2, ASRS, TNFD, China, EU, US, or other framework requirements
- a real company or real project has achieved emissions reduction, removal, offsetting, net zero, nature-positive, climate resilience, or ESG performance
- a comparison result is current without source review, citation, versioning, and Founder / GPT review
- ClimateOS or EcoEngine validation linkage proves real-world impact

Task142 also does not authorize standard-specific factual comparison tables. Any standard-specific content belongs behind the Task143 research protocol and later explicit review gates.

## Comparative Dimensions

The following dimensions define the allowable comparison surface for Task142 and provide controlled inputs for later architecture tasks.

| Dimension | Comparison Question | Allowed Status Values / Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accounting boundary type | What boundary is being described or compared? | Descriptive only; examples may include organizational, operational, product, project, activity, value-chain, temporal, geographic, or system boundary categories without asserting standard-specific rules. |
| Evidence type | What kind of evidence is associated with the accounting claim or method? | Source document, measurement record, activity record, model output, audit / review note, assumption register, uncertainty note, or missing evidence marker. |
| Claim type | What kind of claim is being framed? | Inventory, baseline, reduction, removal, offset, avoided emissions, transition plan, disclosure support, risk context, validation question, or unresolved claim. |
| Method / formula reference status | Is a method, formula, factor, or assumption referenced and reviewable? | Referenced, missing, partial, disputed, superseded, versioned, pending research, or not applicable. This status does not validate correctness. |
| Review status | What review state applies to the comparison artifact or evidence? | Draft, internal review, expert review needed, external source needed, disputed, frozen, superseded, or rejected. No assurance or certification status is created. |
| Jurisdiction / framework context | What future research context may be relevant? | IPCC, ISSB, IFRS S2, ASRS, TNFD, China, EU, US, or other framework contexts may be named only as future research subjects / comparison targets, not interpreted in Task142. |
| Uncertainty / limitation | What is unknown, unstable, missing, or constrained? | Data limitation, boundary limitation, method limitation, temporal limitation, source freshness limitation, applicability limitation, review limitation, or unresolved conflict. |
| ClimateOS / EcoEngine validation linkage | What real-world validation question would follow from the accounting comparison? | Linkage may frame questions about climate, ecological, physical-system, resilience, or risk-reduction effects. It must not assert impact, causality, or validated outcomes. |

## Non-Authoritative Comparison Model

The Task142 model is comparison-by-structure, not comparison-by-authority.

CarbonOS may produce a controlled comparison object with these conceptual fields:

| Field | Purpose | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparison subject | Names the accounting question, claim category, or method category being compared. | Must not use real company / project conclusions in Task142. |
| Boundary descriptor | Records the accounting boundary being discussed. | Descriptive only; no compliance interpretation. |
| Evidence descriptor | Identifies evidence categories and evidence sufficiency questions. | Does not validate evidence or determine sufficiency. |
| Method reference status | Records whether method / formula references exist and whether they need research. | Does not endorse any method, formula, or factor. |
| Context marker | Names the future jurisdiction / framework context for research routing. | Future research subject only. |
| Limitation register | Captures uncertainties, missing sources, and review constraints. | Must remain visible in all later use. |
| Validation linkage question | Connects the comparison to ClimateOS / EcoEngine validation inquiry. | Question-only; no impact conclusion. |
| Review gate | States the review required before any factual comparison is recorded. | Founder / GPT review and source citation required before factual standard claims. |

## Boundary Against Compliance / Assurance / Certification

Task142 creates no compliance, assurance, verification, certification, audit, legal, financial, or public disclosure authority.

CarbonOS comparative accounting architecture may help future reviewers identify:

- which evidence is missing
- which source research is required
- which assumptions need review
- which boundary questions are unresolved
- which claims should not be accepted without further review

It may not decide:

- whether a disclosure is compliant
- whether a claim is assured or certifiable
- whether an accounting method is legally correct
- whether a report satisfies a named standard
- whether a real-world climate or ESG claim is true

## Boundary Against Runtime / API / Database / MCP / Scoring / Automation

Task142 does not create or authorize:

- runtime implementation
- API implementation
- MCP implementation
- database implementation
- scoring implementation
- calculators
- automation
- executable schemas
- data pipelines
- agents
- QCloud dispatch
- external integrations

Any later movement toward operational capability requires a separate explicit approval path and cannot be inferred from this scope map.
