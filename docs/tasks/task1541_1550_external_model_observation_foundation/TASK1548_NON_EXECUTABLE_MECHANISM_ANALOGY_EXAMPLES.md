# Task1548 — Non-Executable Mechanism Analogy Examples

Date: 2026-07-14

Status: ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY / NO LOCATION STUDY

## Purpose

These examples demonstrate how ClimateOS may compare places without declaring them equivalent.

No weather, climate, infrastructure or socioeconomic dataset was acquired. No numerical claim or score is made.

## Example A — Sydney and Shanghai

### Candidate shared mechanisms

- coastal metropolitan exposure;
- maritime influence;
- humid-heat and urban-heat interaction;
- high infrastructure and energy demand;
- flood and drainage relevance;
- compound interaction between regional climate and built form.

### Required difference evidence

- latitude and seasonal cycle;
- regional circulation and monsoon influence;
- ocean-current and sea-surface-temperature context;
- rainfall seasonality and storm types;
- topography and catchment geometry;
- urban density and building form;
- infrastructure standards;
- water, energy and planning governance;
- observation systems and data comparability.

### Decision

CANDIDATE STRUCTURAL COMPARISON / NOT AN ANALOGUE YET

The shared coastal-city label is insufficient. Each mechanism requires its own Observation Frame and evidence.

## Example B — Alice Springs and Karamay

### Candidate shared mechanisms

- inland and arid environmental constraints;
- high water-resource relevance;
- strong continental or land-surface influence;
- large diurnal or seasonal temperature considerations;
- infrastructure dependence on constrained environmental systems.

### Required difference evidence

- latitude and elevation;
- winter cold and summer heat regimes;
- atmospheric circulation;
- desert and land-surface characteristics;
- water-source engineering;
- industrial structure and land use;
- population and urban morphology;
- governance and resource history;
- data period and quality.

### Decision

CANDIDATE MECHANISM ANALOGY / NOT AN EQUIVALENCE

The word arid does not establish transferability.

## Required analogy workflow

1. define each place independently;
2. choose one candidate mechanism;
3. align spatial and temporal scales;
4. list comparable variables;
5. list structural differences and confounders;
6. identify external drivers;
7. assess uncertainty and non-stationarity;
8. record transfer limits;
9. obtain relevant human scientific review;
10. admit, reject or defer the analogy.

## Stop rules

Reject or defer an analogy if:

- it relies on place labels;
- one variable dominates the comparison;
- the time periods differ materially;
- local drivers are omitted;
- a national or continental average substitutes for locality evidence;
- correlation is described as mechanism;
- the comparison would support an operational or policy claim without review.
