## 3. Why this case belongs under Task167

Task167 should not remain abstract. Heat resilience must eventually be tested against real urban systems.

Yujin Yang’s article is useful because it shows heat resilience as a **system of systems**, not a single intervention:

- street trees and parks reduce heat exposure;
- sponge-city measures support moisture retention and thermal moderation;
- underground spaces can serve as emergency cooling refuges;
- care stations protect outdoor workers;
- meteorological and health-risk warnings connect climate data to public health;
- digital tools can guide citizens toward cooling resources.

This makes the case suitable for ClimateOS because ClimateOS needs evidence chains, not slogans.

## 4. ESG Shadow Logic

This file adds an ESG “shadow,” meaning a light but traceable ESG interpretation layer. It does not convert Task167 into an ESG task.

### 4.1 Environmental shadow

Urban cooling has environmental relevance when it reduces:

- urban heat island exposure;
- excessive cooling-energy demand;
- drought-heat feedback;
- surface heat storage;
- ecosystem fragmentation.

Potential evidence fields:

```text
tree canopy coverage
green-space accessibility
surface temperature change
permeable surface ratio
soil moisture retention
blue-green corridor continuity
```

### 4.2 Social shadow

Urban cooling becomes an ESG++ social issue when it protects groups with higher heat exposure or lower adaptive capacity.

Relevant groups:

```text

children
outdoor workers
delivery riders

construction workers
low-income households

```

Potential evidence fields:

```text

```

### 4.3 Governance shadow

Urban cooling becomes a governance issue when cities can show who maintains, activates, funds, monitors, and evaluates cooling infrastructure.

Potential evidence fields:

