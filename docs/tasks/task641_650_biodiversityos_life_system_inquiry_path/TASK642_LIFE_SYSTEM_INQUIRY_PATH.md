# Task642 Life System Inquiry Path

## Purpose

Describe the plain-language inquiry path for Life System and BiodiversityOS
work.

## Core Idea

ClimateOS should not begin BiodiversityOS by asking for a score. It should
begin by asking what living systems are doing, what signals can be observed,
what might be changing, and how those changes may matter for climate
resilience.

## Inquiry Path

1. Notice a living-world signal.

Examples: canopy loss, wetland stress, reduced pollinator activity, soil
degradation, habitat fragmentation, invasive species pressure, or reduced
ecological recovery after disturbance.

2. Ask why it may matter.

The signal may affect heat, water, carbon, soil stability, recovery capacity,
food systems, community wellbeing, or governance risk.

3. Separate observation from interpretation.

Observation says what was seen. Interpretation says what it may mean. ClimateOS
must keep these apart.

4. Form a cautious hypothesis.

The system may ask: could this biodiversity change be related to heat
resilience, flood buffering, drought vulnerability, carbon storage, or
ecosystem recovery?

5. Identify evidence needs.

The next step is not a conclusion. It is a search for evidence, baselines,
methods, time windows, spatial boundaries, and alternative explanations.

6. Review uncertainty.

ClimateOS should ask what is unknown, what may be confounded, what requires
domain expertise, and what should not be claimed.

7. Record learning.

The output is a learning record: what is known, what is suspected, what is
uncertain, what should be observed next, and what requires human review.

## Current Capability

This path is conceptual. It does not implement sensing, modeling, scoring,
forecasting, or automated ecological assessment.
