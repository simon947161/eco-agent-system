# Task612-613 Evidence-Grounded Deliberation Principles

## Purpose

Define how ClimateOS should support deliberation across domains without
claiming automated decision authority.

## Principle 1: Evidence Before Preference

Deliberation should begin by identifying what evidence exists, what evidence is
missing, and which claims the evidence can or cannot support.

## Principle 2: Uncertainty Remains Visible

Uncertainty, missing data, disagreement, stale records, and weak source
authority must remain visible during deliberation. They should not be hidden by
summaries or design polish.

## Principle 3: Affected Humans Stay In The Loop

Human review, community context, domain experts, and Founder Gates remain
necessary where evidence affects governance direction, resource priorities, or
public claims.

## Principle 4: Deliberation Is Not Certification

ClimateOS deliberation can organize evidence and questions. It does not certify
environmental outcomes, compliance, assurance, scientific truth, or financial
value.

## Principle 5: Disagreement Is Preserved

The system should preserve minority signals, domain disagreements, and
conflicting interpretations until they are resolved or explicitly escalated.

## Principle 6: Action Requires Boundary Clarity

Before a proposed action is recorded as ready for governance review, the record
should state domain scope, evidence basis, uncertainty, human review state, and
prohibited claims.

## Risks

- Strong narrative may outrun evidence.
- Model-assisted text may sound more certain than the record supports.
- Cross-domain summaries may flatten specialist caveats.
- Human authority may drift into passive approval unless review roles are
  explicit.

## Current Capability

These principles are documentation only. No deliberation engine, agent,
workflow automation, scoring system, or decision system is implemented.
