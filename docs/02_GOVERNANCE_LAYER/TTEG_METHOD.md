## 1. Purpose

This document defines TTEG as a core governance method inside CCZPS 2.0.

TTEG stands for:

**Thermo-Temporal Environment Governance**

It focuses on the relationship between:

- heat,
- time,
- water,
- wind,
- soil,
- vegetation,
- human control.

The purpose is to move climate governance beyond static carbon accounting and toward dynamic environmental rhythm management.

---

## 2. Core Problem

Many climate and ecological failures are not caused only by the total amount of carbon, heat, or water.

They are often caused by timing mismatch.

Examples:

- vegetation starts growth before snowmelt water arrives,
- summer water arrives too late and evaporates rapidly,
- urban heat accumulates at night and blocks cooling cycles,
- irrigation occurs at the wrong thermal window,
- wind and heat amplify evaporation during sensitive periods,
- industrial heat sources disturb local air movement and humidity.

Therefore, governance must ask not only:

> How much heat?  
> How much water?  
> How much carbon?

but also:

> When does it occur?  
> How long does it last?  
> Does the ecosystem have time to absorb it?  
> Is the system rhythm becoming mismatched?

---

## 3. Core Definition

TTEG is a governance method for identifying and managing heat-time-environment mismatch.

It treats climate adaptation as a dynamic timing problem.

The central idea is:

> Climate governance is not only carbon reduction.  
> It is the management of heat, time, water, and ecological response rhythms.

---

## 4. Three Core Dimensions

### 4.1 Thermal Dimension

The thermal dimension identifies heat sources, heat accumulation, heat transfer, and heat disturbance.

It includes:

- urban heat island,
- industrial heat forcing,
- solar radiation,
- surface temperature,
- night heat retention,
- dry-hot wind,
- building and infrastructure heat release.

### 4.2 Temporal Dimension

The temporal dimension identifies timing, duration, rhythm, lag, and sequence.

It includes:

- day-night temperature change,
- seasonal water arrival,
- drought legacy,
- vegetation recovery lag,
- snowmelt delay,
- heatwave duration,
- irrigation timing,
- energy-dispatch timing.

### 4.3 Environmental Dimension

The environmental dimension identifies system response.

It includes:

- soil moisture,
- vegetation stress,
- evaporation pressure,
- water availability,
- humidity redistribution,
- ecological resilience,
- salinity accumulation,
- fire risk.

## 5. Simplified Engineering Expression

A simplified TTEG expression can be written as:

```text
R(t) = f{ΔT(t), V(t), RH(t), S(t), C(t)}
```

Where:

- R(t) = system water-resource accessibility or regeneration condition at time t,
- ΔT(t) = day-night or seasonal temperature difference,
- V(t) = wind speed,
- RH(t) = relative humidity,
- S(t) = solar input,
- C(t) = human or AI control strategy.

This expression is not a final scientific model.

It is an engineering judgment framework for climate-adaptive planning.

---

## 6. TTEG in Dryland Systems

Dryland systems are highly sensitive to heat-time mismatch.

Typical problems include:

- rainfall does not arrive during ecological demand windows,
- snowmelt arrives after early vegetation stress,
- wind increases evaporation during hot periods,
- soil mineralization and salinity accelerate under heat stress,
- infrastructure water supply ignores ecological timing.

TTEG helps identify:

- when water should be stored,
- when water should be released,
- when vegetation needs thermal protection,
- when evaporation risk is highest,
- when artificial cooling or shading should be activated.

---

## 7. TTEG in Urban and Building Systems

In towns, buildings, and urban districts, TTEG can guide:

- night purge ventilation,
- solar chimney operation,
- semi-open buffer spaces,
- public shade timing,
- cooling corridor design,
- heatwave emergency response,
- AI-based building-climate control.

A building or town should not be treated as a sealed object.

It should be treated as a climate participant that exchanges heat, air, water, and energy with its environment.

## 9. Relationship with Possibility Computing

Possibility Computing compares multiple futures.

TTEG provides one of the key comparison logics:

- Which scenario reduces heat-time mismatch?
- Which scenario improves ecological absorption timing?
- Which scenario stores water before peak evaporation?
- Which scenario reduces night heat accumulation?
- Which scenario supports long-term rhythm recovery?

Therefore, TTEG is the temporal intelligence layer of CCZPS.

---

## 11. Human Governance Boundary

TTEG may support automated warnings and adaptive control.

However, major environmental interventions should remain under human governance oversight.

AI may suggest timing.

Human governance must approve major changes.

---

## 12. Final Statement

TTEG reframes climate governance as the management of ecological rhythm.

Climate systems fail not only when resources are insufficient.

They also fail when heat, water, wind, vegetation, and human action lose their timing relationship.

TTEG provides CCZPS 2.0 with the temporal logic needed to compare and govern possible futures.
