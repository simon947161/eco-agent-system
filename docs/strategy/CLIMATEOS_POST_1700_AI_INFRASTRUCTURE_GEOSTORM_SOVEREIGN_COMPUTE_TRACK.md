# ClimateOS Post-1700 Workflow

## AI-Informed GeoStorm, Data-Centre Infrastructure & Sovereign Compute Track

**Status:** Founder-directed future research and business-development workflow  
**Activation window:** After the current ClimateOS task sequence reaches the post-1700 planning gate  
**Repository role:** Durable strategic instruction for future ClimateOS task planning  
**Decision authority:** Human-reviewed; no autonomous planning, engineering, legal, investment or approval conclusions

## 1. Founder intent

ClimateOS shall establish a post-1700 research and development workflow connecting:

- Australian AI policy and cross-government coordination;
- sovereign AI and sovereign computing capacity;
- data-centre planning, environmental assessment and infrastructure delivery;
- electricity generation, grid connection, transmission and renewable-energy additionality;
- water demand, cooling, wastewater, recycled-water and drought constraints;
- carbon, embodied emissions, climate disclosure and evidence governance;
- climate hazards, severe wind, cyclone, geomagnetic storm and space-weather resilience;
- the existing ClimateOS model-comparison, regional-risk and Evidence Passport architecture;
- practical environmental-planning and advisory business opportunities.

This workflow records the Founder’s view that AI is not only a software or GPU issue. It is becoming an energy, water, land-use, resilience, planning and national-infrastructure system.

## 2. Working title

**AI-Informed GeoStorm, Data-Centre Infrastructure & Sovereign Compute Track**

The title may later be shortened, but the workflow must retain all three linked domains:

1. AI infrastructure and data centres;
2. climate, cyclone, severe-wind and geomagnetic/space-weather resilience;
3. sovereign compute, public policy and evidence-based environmental governance.

## 3. Strategic trigger

The track is prompted by emerging Australian policy signals that AI development will be actively supported but governed across departments, and that major data-centre growth must be considered together with energy-system contribution, grid costs, water use, environmental impacts and national interest.

Speeches and policy announcements must be stored as strategic signals, not treated as operative law until supported by legislation, regulation, standards, formal programs, planning instruments, procurement rules or approval conditions.

## 4. Existing case linkage

ClimateOS already contains or has previously processed an Australian data-centre environmental impact assessment case as a cross-OS evidence and learning case. The post-1700 track must not duplicate or discard that work.

At activation, the first preflight shall:

1. locate the existing data-centre EIS/EIA case and its repository records;
2. identify its project name, jurisdiction, assessment pathway, source documents and evidence status;
3. link it into this track as the initial practical case;
4. extract reusable evidence categories without importing unsupported conclusions;
5. preserve the boundary between ClimateOS evidence interpretation and BuildingOS project/design authority.

The case should be used to study, where evidence permits:

- electricity demand and grid connection;
- renewable-energy commitments and additionality;
- backup generation and storage;
- water consumption and cooling systems;
- wastewater, recycled water and drought exposure;
- embodied and operational carbon;
- land use, biodiversity, noise, heat and local amenity;
- bushfire, flood, heatwave, cyclone, severe-wind and space-weather resilience;
- cumulative impacts and conditions of consent;
- community, Traditional Owner and regional-development implications;
- evidence gaps between project claims and verifiable performance.

## 5. Core research questions

### 5.1 Policy and governance

- What formal role will the Australian Government AI Office, or any successor body, have?
- How will Commonwealth, state, energy, environment, planning, water, industry, security and treasury functions be coordinated?
- Which policy statements become binding instruments, funding rules, procurement conditions or approval requirements?
- How will sovereign AI, data sovereignty, critical infrastructure and regional development be defined?

### 5.2 Energy and grid

- Must new data centres fund new generation, storage, network augmentation or grid connection costs?
- How will renewable-energy additionality be demonstrated?
- Can a data centre be assessed as a net contributor to energy-system capacity or resilience rather than merely a large load?
- How should hourly matching, firming, backup power and Scope 2 claims be evidenced?

### 5.3 Water and heat

- What are the full water footprints of cooling options under Australian climate conditions?
- When should recycled water, dry cooling, hybrid cooling, heat recovery or district energy be required?
- How should drought, heatwave and catchment constraints affect siting and approval?

### 5.4 Climate and geophysical resilience

- How should flood, bushfire, extreme heat, cyclone, severe wind and smoke exposure affect site selection and design assumptions?
- What dependencies exist between data centres, substations, transmission, telecommunications and emergency systems?
- How should geomagnetic storms, solar events and broader space-weather risks be translated into bounded infrastructure-resilience evidence?
- Which claims require specialist electrical, structural, space-weather or critical-infrastructure review?

### 5.5 Environmental evidence and accountability

- What evidence should support energy, carbon, water, biodiversity, resilience and community-benefit claims?
- Can each facility or compute cluster receive an AI Infrastructure Evidence Passport?
- How can measured performance be compared with EIS commitments, approval conditions and public claims?
- How can ClimateOS, CarbonOS, WaterOS, BuildingOS and ECOChain share evidence without transferring professional decision authority?

## 6. Proposed ClimateOS work packages after Task 1700

Exact task numbers must be assigned only after reviewing the authoritative task index and active sequence. The following is a workflow structure, not automatic implementation authorization.

### Work Package A — Policy and institutional map

- Australian AI Office and cross-government coordination map;
- Commonwealth/state responsibility matrix;
- policy-to-law and announcement-to-implementation tracker;
- sovereign compute and critical-infrastructure definitions;
- relevant funding, procurement and regional-development programs.

### Work Package B — Data-centre EIS evidence model

- locate and register the existing Australian data-centre EIS case;
- create a reusable source and claim taxonomy;
- map energy, water, carbon, biodiversity, planning and resilience evidence;
- distinguish applicant claims, regulator findings, approval conditions and measured outcomes;
- define evidence gaps and human-review requirements.

### Work Package C — AI Infrastructure Evidence Passport v0.1

Candidate sections:

- identity, ownership, location and jurisdiction;
- compute capacity and operating profile;
- grid connection, generation, storage and backup systems;
- renewable-energy sourcing and additionality;
- water source, cooling technology and discharge;
- operational and embodied carbon;
- land, biodiversity, heat, noise and community impacts;
- climate and geophysical hazards;
- approval conditions, commitments and monitoring records;
- source provenance, confidence and specialist review.

### Work Package D — AI energy and water coupling

- hourly energy matching and firming logic;
- grid constraints and network augmentation evidence;
- water-energy-carbon trade-off model;
- waste-heat recovery and productive-use scenarios;
- regional carrying-capacity and cumulative-impact framework.

### Work Package E — GeoStorm and extreme-hazard resilience

- geomagnetic-storm and space-weather evidence register;
- dependency map for transmission, substations, telecommunications and compute;
- cyclone, severe-wind, flood, bushfire and heatwave coupling;
- scenario and stress-test templates;
- explicit specialist-review and uncertainty gates.

### Work Package F — Planning and decision-support interface

- siting-screen evidence layers;
- EIS/SSD/DA evidence intake template;
- approval-condition monitoring view;
- public-claim versus measured-performance comparison;
- Council, state, Commonwealth and infrastructure-provider use cases.

### Work Package G — Business and partnership pathway

- environmental-planning and evidence-governance service definition;
- data-centre EIS review and evidence-gap assessment offer;
- AI infrastructure climate-risk and resilience assessment offer;
- regional energy-water-compute opportunity screening;
- potential collaboration map across government, universities, CSIRO, energy networks, water utilities, councils, developers and engineering firms;
- grant, pilot and procurement opportunity register.

## 7. Cross-OS responsibilities

### ClimateOS

Climate hazard interpretation, model evidence, regional context, uncertainty, scenario comparison and resilience evidence.

### CarbonOS

Operational and embodied carbon, emissions factors, disclosure mapping, energy claims and carbon credentials.

### WaterOS

Water sourcing, cooling demand, recycled water, discharge, drought, catchment constraints and water evidence.

### BuildingOS

Building, MEP, BIM, construction, operations, lifecycle and approval-condition evidence. BuildingOS retains its own professional and governance boundaries.

### ECOChain / Evidence Passport

Source provenance, claim lineage, credentials, verification status, review records and longitudinal performance evidence.

No OS may silently inherit another OS’s conclusions or professional authority.

## 10. Governance boundaries

This document authorizes future research planning and durable roadmap recognition only. It does not by itself authorize:

- production implementation;
- live data acquisition requiring payment, accounts or restricted licences;
- engineering design or certification;
- statutory planning or legal conclusions;
- contacting agencies, developers, utilities or experts;
- investment, site-selection or project-feasibility conclusions;
- public claims that a project is sustainable, resilient, net-energy-positive or compliant.

All high-consequence conclusions require traceable sources, uncertainty statements and competent human review.
