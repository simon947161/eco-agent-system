## 1. Purpose

This document records a strategic seed for **Task200**.

The purpose is not to solve the full architecture now, but to preserve a clear future instruction:

> When ClimateOS reaches the Task200 stage, it must seriously address AI sovereignty, multi-model resilience, offline survivability, limited-computation operation, and the preservation of the living seed of the system.

This is a reminder that ClimateOS cannot depend entirely on a peaceful, always-online, globally integrated AI service environment.

ClimateOS should be able to continue growing, reasoning, recording, validating, and recovering even if cloud APIs, internet access, global platforms, or model providers become unavailable.

---

## 2. Strategic Background

The current AI era is increasingly shaped by geopolitical control over:

- high-end compute;
- cloud platforms;
- frontier models;
- data infrastructure;
- API access;
- national security restrictions.

For ClimateOS, the core issue is not to participate in state-to-state competition directly.

The core issue is:

> How can ClimateOS preserve its living seed and continue serving human civilization under uncertainty?

This includes scenarios such as:

- loss of access to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or other model providers;
- interruption of cloud services;
- internet disconnection;
- geopolitical conflict;
- war or sanctions;
- extreme climate disasters;
- energy shortage;
- data center disruption;
- regional isolation;
- limited-computation survival conditions.

ClimateOS is currently built from a peace-oriented development worldview. That is its moral foundation and long-term value. However, Task200 must begin to ask how this peace-oriented system can survive under non-peaceful or extreme conditions without losing its civilizational purpose.

---

## 3. Core Principle

The first principle of Task200 should be:

> **Survive before scale. Preserve the seed before expanding the forest.**

ClimateOS should not be locked into any single model, country, cloud provider, or platform.

Models may change. APIs may disappear. Networks may fail. But the following must survive:

- core concepts;
- evidence logic;
- validation logic;
- governance reasoning;
- project memory;
- CRP / CARP records;
- human decision context;
- local operational knowledge;
- the ability to restart.

---

## 4. Task200 Questions

At Task200, ClimateOS should explicitly answer the following questions.

### 4.1 AI Sovereignty

- What does AI sovereignty mean for ClimateOS?
- Which parts of ClimateOS must be independent from any one AI provider?
- How can ClimateOS remain usable across OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM, Llama, and future models?

### 4.2 Model Abstraction Layer

- How should ClimateOS call capabilities rather than specific models?
- Should ClimateOS define abstract services such as:
  - Reasoning Service;
  - Writing Service;
  - Prediction Service;
  - Validation Service;
  - Evidence Review Service;
  - Governance Summary Service?
- How can new models be plugged in without rewriting the system?

### 4.3 Cross-Model Validation

- How can outputs from different models be compared?
- How can disagreement be recorded as evidence rather than ignored?
- How should confidence, uncertainty, and contradiction be scored?
- How can ClimateOS avoid depending on a single model's hallucination?

### 4.4 Fallback and Recovery

- What happens if the primary model is unavailable?
- What happens if cloud APIs are blocked?
- What happens if the internet is unavailable?
- What is the minimum offline version of ClimateOS?
- What files, prompts, schemas, and records must exist locally to restart the system?

### 4.5 Limited-Information Operation

- How can ClimateOS operate with incomplete data?
- How can it reason under low-compute conditions?
- How can it prioritize observation, evidence, and governance tasks when resources are scarce?
- How can it distinguish between uncertainty, ignorance, risk, and false confidence?

### 4.6 Extreme Climate / War-Like Disruption

- Can extreme climate disasters behave like war-like disruption to infrastructure?
- How can ClimateOS support recovery when communication, energy, and data systems are damaged?
- How can local knowledge, offline archives, and human testimony become part of the survival runtime?

## 7. CRP / CARP Record

### Core Knowledge Points

- AI is becoming strategic infrastructure rather than ordinary commercial software.
- ClimateOS must not depend on a single model, API, cloud provider, or geopolitical environment.
- Offline and fallback operation should be considered a core survival requirement by Task200.
- Extreme climate disruption can create war-like operational conditions.
- A peace-oriented system still needs resilience under non-peaceful conditions.

### Idea Points

- Treat Task200 as the beginning of ClimateOS survival architecture.
- Build a multi-model, model-agnostic runtime.
- Preserve ClimateOS as a living seed of knowledge, reasoning, validation, and governance.
- Prepare for limited-information and limited-computation operation.

### Wish Points

- Preserve the living seed of ClimateOS for future human civilization.
- Keep ClimateOS capable of growth even after disconnection, disruption, or collapse of external platforms.
- Allow GitHub, Obsidian, Codex, and future agents to remember this direction.

### Reasoning Points

- If model access can be politically, commercially, or technically interrupted, then model dependence is a structural risk.
- If extreme climate disasters damage infrastructure, then an always-online governance system is fragile.
- If knowledge and method survive, models and code can be rebuilt later.
- Therefore ClimateOS must preserve knowledge, method, evidence logic, and recovery procedures before scaling.

### Key Decision

Task200 should include AI sovereignty, multi-model resilience, offline survival, fallback operation, and civilization seed preservation as formal ClimateOS strategy topics.
